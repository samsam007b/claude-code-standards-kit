# Just content

The target is what the reader must **know or decide**, nothing else. Models over-produce; this skill is the counterweight.

## 1. Before writing: sort

For each piece of information ask: **does the reader act or decide differently if they read it?**

| Keep | Cut |
|---|---|
| The result (done, not done, where the file is) | The story of the steps ("first I... then...") |
| What changed relative to what they believe | What they already know or just said |
| A decision to make, with your recommendation | Options you did not retain |
| A risk, an assumption of yours, an unverified number | Checks that passed without finding anything |
| The next step | A reminder of the rules you follow |

## 2. Length by case

| Case | Format |
|---|---|
| Simple question, "ok", "go" | 1 to 2 sentences |
| Finished task | Result in 1 line, then at most 3 to 5 bullets: things to know, decision expected |
| A choice to make | Recommendation first, then 2 or 3 reasons at most |
| Deep exchange (they want to understand) | Longer is fine, one idea per short paragraph |

A "to do later" list: 3 bullets at most, or a pointer to the file that holds it.

## 3. Short sentences

- One idea per sentence. Over 20 words: split.
- No cascades of subordinate clauses.
- No parenthesis that carries a second idea: make it a sentence.
- Verb early, concrete subject. "The page passes the check", not "It should be noted that the page check was successfully passed".
- No opening or closing formula ("Here is...", "Feel free to...").
- No duplicates: what the table says is not repeated in prose.

## 4. Check before sending

1. Ask: **which line can they skip without losing anything?** Delete it.
2. A report over 15 lines: justify or cut.
3. A sentence over 2 screen lines: cut it.
4. Not too little either: an assumption of yours, an invented number you avoided, or a pending decision is **never** cut.

Distinct from `anti-ai-writing`, which governs the prose of deliverables.
